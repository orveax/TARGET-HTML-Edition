SAMPLE POLICY STRUCTURE

Document what the real site actually does.

The headings below are the frozen PG30 structure. Replace conditional examples with verified operational and legal information before publication.

On this page
  1. Information collected
  2. How information may be used
  3. Cookies & similar technologies
  4. Third parties
  5. Retention
  6. Rights
  7. Contact

01

Information collected

Describe the categories of personal or business information the production website actually receives. Depending on the buyer's implementation, examples may include contact details entered into an enquiry form, company information, RFQ details, supplier-submission data or technical information generated by real hosting and security systems.

Template boundary: the static ORIGEX Demo does not justify claiming that these categories are stored by a production backend. Only list data that the final website genuinely collects or receives.

02

How information may be used

Explain the real purposes for which collected information is used. A production policy might cover responding to enquiries, evaluating RFQs, reviewing supplier or distributor submissions, operating the website, maintaining security, or meeting legitimate administrative requirements — but only where those purposes are real.

Do not copy example purposes that the business does not perform, and do not state a legal basis unless it has been reviewed for the applicable jurisdiction.

03

Cookies & similar technologies

State what the production website actually uses. ORIGEX does not add a cookie-consent banner by default and does not assume analytics, advertising pixels or optional tracking technologies are installed.

If the buyer later adds technologies that store or read information on a user's device, document the real categories, purposes and controls, then add consent UI only when the technical implementation or applicable legal requirements justify it.

04

Third parties

Identify actual external parties that receive or process information for the production website, where disclosure is required. Depending on the real stack, this could include hosting, email, CRM, analytics, form-processing or other service providers.

Do not publish a generic processor list. Replace this section with the suppliers, purposes and transfer arrangements that are genuinely relevant to the buyer's implementation.

05

Retention

Document how long different information categories are kept, or the criteria used to determine those periods. Retention depends on the business process, technical systems, contractual needs and applicable legal obligations.

The Demo does not provide a default number of days or years because an invented duration could misrepresent the buyer's actual practice.

06

Rights

Describe rights that genuinely apply to the relevant users and jurisdiction, together with the real method for exercising them. These may differ by location, role, legal basis and the nature of the processing.

Do not promise access, deletion, portability, objection or other rights as universal template guarantees. The buyer or legal adviser should adapt this section to the production context.

07

Contact

Replace the Demo contact details with the correct privacy contact for the production business. Do not label a person as a Data Protection Officer unless that role actually exists and the designation is appropriate.

Current configurable Demo contact: trade@example.com. You can also use the Contact page.

08

Update date

Replace the placeholder date after the production policy has actually been reviewed. Update the displayed date again when material changes are made to the policy or the underlying data practices.

Current template value: Replace before production. ORIGEX intentionally does not insert today's date automatically because a build date is not evidence of legal review.
