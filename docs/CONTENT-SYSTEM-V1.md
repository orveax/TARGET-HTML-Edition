# ORIGEX — Content System V1

Product ID: ORX-P01  
Owner: ORVEAX  
Status: APPROVED & LOCKED  
Authority: Canonical content-writing and content-extension system for ORIGEX V1 and future feature additions.

## 1. Core Principle

ORIGEX does not use filler copy, generic corporate language, or page-first writing.

Every content unit must answer a real commercial question for one or more of these audiences:
- Buyer / Procurement.
- Supplier / Manufacturer / Brand Owner.
- Distributor / Commercial Partner.
- Trading / Market-Access Team.

Arabic is the master commercial language. English is a professional adaptation, not a literal translation.

Core rule:

> Define the commercial intent once → write the Arabic master → adapt to English → add states and microcopy → freeze → implement.

No page or Additional Feature may enter implementation without a completed Content Contract.

---

## 2. ORIGEX Commercial Voice

### Voice
- Clear.
- Commercial.
- Practical.
- Credible.
- B2B.
- Informed by food trading, distribution, supplier qualification, product data and market access.

### Tone
- Confident without exaggerated claims.
- Helpful without sounding consumer-retail.
- Specific without unnecessary jargon.
- Premium without decorative language.
- Direct about requirements, next steps and commercial boundaries.

### Avoid
- “World-class”, “best-in-class”, “revolutionary”, “leading global company” unless factual and user-supplied.
- Fake metrics, fake customers, fake certifications, fake years of experience.
- Restaurant or hospitality language when the page is about trade/distribution.
- Empty phrases such as “we deliver excellence”.
- Literal Arabic-to-English translation that damages natural English business language.

## 3. Master Commercial Message

### Arabic
**من المصدر إلى السوق، بمسار تجاري أوضح.**

نربط المصنعين والموردين والمشترين وشركاء التوزيع من خلال معلومات منتج واضحة، تقييم تجاري منظم، ومسار عملي من فرصة التوريد إلى الوصول للسوق.

### English
**From source to market, through a clearer commercial route.**

We connect manufacturers, suppliers, buyers and distribution partners through clear product information, structured commercial evaluation and a practical route from supply opportunity to market access.

## 4. Audience Writing Model

### A — Buyer / Procurement
Primary questions:
- What products are available?
- What is the origin?
- What is the MOQ?
- What is the pack size?
- What is the shelf life?
- What documents are available?
- How do I request pricing?

Content priorities:
Product clarity → commercial facts → availability → documentation → RFQ.

### B — Supplier / Manufacturer
Primary questions:
- Is my product suitable?
- What information is required?
- What markets/channels are relevant?
- How do I submit a product?
- What happens after submission?

Content priorities:
Fit → qualification → required information → process → commercial next step.

### C — Distributor / Partner
Primary questions:
- What kind of partnership is being considered?
- Which territories/channels matter?
- What capabilities should I provide?
- How is fit assessed?

Content priorities:
Territory → channel → portfolio → operational capability → partnership review.

### D — Trading / Market-Access Team
Primary questions:
- Is the opportunity commercially coherent?
- Are product data complete?
- Are responsibilities clear?
- Is the next decision actionable?

Content priorities:
Structured information → decision support → commercial boundaries → next action.

## 6. Writing Anatomy

### Hero
1. Eyebrow / category context.
2. One clear H1.
3. 1–2 sentence commercial explanation.
4. One primary CTA.
5. Maximum one secondary CTA.
6. Optional factual proof/data — never invented.

### Standard Section
1. Kicker / context.
2. Outcome-led heading.
3. Short explanatory paragraph.
4. Structured content: cards / facts / steps / table.
5. Optional action.

### Card
1. Semantic label/icon.
2. Outcome or entity title.
3. One clear explanatory line.
4. Commercial metadata where applicable.
5. Explicit action where needed.

### Form
1. Explain why the form exists.
2. Tell the user what information helps.
3. Use visible labels.
4. Explain attachments and limits.
5. State what happens after submission.
6. Never imply a live backend in the static demo.

## 9. Product Writing Rules

Product copy is data-led.

Mandatory fields where applicable:
- Product name.
- Brand/demo brand.
- Category.
- Origin.
- Pack size.
- Case pack/packaging.
- Shelf life.
- Storage.
- MOQ.
- Availability.
- Certifications/document availability.
- Supplier reference.

Do not use unsupported quality claims.

Preferred pattern:

**Arabic:**
“منتج غذائي معبأ مناسب لقنوات التجزئة والجملة، مع بيانات تعبئة وتخزين واضحة لدعم التقييم التجاري الأولي.”

**English:**
“A packaged food product suitable for retail and wholesale channels, presented with clear packing and storage information for initial commercial evaluation.”

---

## 10. Supplier Writing Rules

Supplier copy must describe:
- What the supplier/manufacturer makes.
- Country of origin.
- Product categories.
- Documentation/certifications as demo placeholders only unless real.
- Target markets/channels.
- Commercial relationship context.

Never imply exclusive agency or representation unless factual and supplied by the buyer of the template.

---

## 11. Market Writing Rules

Market content in the demo is illustrative, not market research.

Every demo market page or market-data block must use a visible disclaimer when presenting non-factual sample content:

Arabic:
“بيانات السوق في هذا العرض توضيحية لأغراض القالب ويجب استبدالها ببيانات السوق الفعلية قبل النشر.”

English:
“Market information in this demo is illustrative template content and should be replaced with verified market data before publication.”

---

## 12. Certification & Compliance Writing Rules

Demo certifications must be labelled as examples/placeholders.

Arabic:
“مثال توضيحي لنوع مستند امتثال يمكن عرضه هنا. لا يمثل شهادة فعلية لشركة حقيقية.”

English:
“Illustrative example of a compliance document type. It does not represent an actual certification held by a real company.”

No fake certificate numbers, issuers, audit dates or claims.

---

## 13. Case Study Writing Rules

Case studies may be realistic demo scenarios but must be clearly marked Demo Case Study.

Structure:
- Context.
- Challenge.
- Commercial objective.
- Approach.
- Decision path.
- Illustrative result.
- What the template user should replace.

Metrics must be labelled illustrative where invented for layout demonstration.

## 14. Microcopy System

### Search
Arabic placeholder: “ابحث بالمنتج أو العلامة أو بلد المنشأ”  
English: “Search by product, brand or origin”

No results AR: “لا توجد نتائج مطابقة. جرّب تعديل البحث أو إزالة بعض الفلاتر.”  
No results EN: “No matching results. Try changing your search or clearing some filters.”

### File Upload
AR: “أرفق ملف المواصفات أو الملف التعريفي عند الحاجة.”  
EN: “Attach a specification sheet or company profile where relevant.”

### Static Form Demo
AR: “هذا نموذج توضيحي. اربطه بخدمة معالجة النماذج أو الـbackend الخاص بك قبل النشر.”  
EN: “This is a demo form. Connect it to your form service or backend before publishing.”

### Success
AR: “تم تجهيز الطلب في العرض التوضيحي. اربط النموذج بنظامك لإرسال الطلبات فعليًا.”  
EN: “The demo submission is complete. Connect the form to your system to process live enquiries.”

### Validation
AR required: “هذا الحقل مطلوب.”  
EN required: “This field is required.”

AR email: “أدخل بريدًا إلكترونيًا صحيحًا.”  
EN email: “Enter a valid email address.”

## 15. Bilingual Adaptation Rules

Arabic master:
- Natural modern business Arabic.
- Short paragraphs.
- Avoid over-formal government language.
- B2B terms may remain English where the market normally uses them.

English adaptation:
- Rewrite for natural B2B English.
- Preserve commercial intent, not sentence structure.
- Avoid inflated marketing copy.
- Keep CTA actions explicit.

A bilingual pair fails QA if one language contains materially different commercial promises, facts or actions.

---

## 16. Content Length Controls

Recommended limits:
- Eyebrow: 2–6 words.
- H1: ideally 4–12 words EN / concise equivalent AR.
- Hero support: 20–45 words.
- Section heading: 4–12 words.
- Section intro: 20–60 words.
- Card title: 2–8 words.
- Card support: 12–35 words.
- Button: 1–5 words.
- Form helper: 8–25 words.

These are editorial controls, not hard technical limits.

## 18. Additional Feature Rule

Every Additional Feature, whether already in V1.1 backlog or proposed later, must receive:
1. Feature purpose.
2. Target audience.
3. Commercial user story.
4. Full Arabic copy.
5. Full English adaptation.
6. Labels and actions.
7. All relevant states.
8. Demo data/sample content.
9. Disclosure/legal copy where relevant.
10. Component/data dependencies.
11. QA acceptance criteria.

An Additional Feature is not considered designed until both UI Contract and Content Contract exist.

---

## 19. Single-Mechanism Rule

Main Features and Additional Features use the same writing mechanism.

There is no separate “quick copy” path for small additions.

If a future feature is added, the required sequence is:

```text
Feature Proposal
→ Scope Classification
→ Content Contract
→ Data Requirements
→ Component/Pattern Selection
→ AR Master
→ EN Adaptation
→ Content Freeze
→ UI Implementation
→ Content QA
```

This rule exists specifically to prevent future additions from feeling bolted-on or underwritten.

---

## 20. Change Control

After this lock:
- Copy correction that preserves meaning = editorial fix.
- New factual data = data update.
- New CTA or user action = content/UX change review.
- New audience promise = commercial content change.
- New feature = new Content Contract before implementation.
